## Findings

- **F01 | I1** — DSD lists three systems supporting staff in flood monitoring and forecasting: Hydrometric Information System, Smart Flood Forecast and Alert System, and AI Flood Monitoring System; context is the "just-in-time clearance arrangement" against extreme-weather flooding.
  Locator: S09 | PDF p.1 (printed p.328) | Reply, para. 1
  Anchor: "including the Hydrometric Information System, the Smart Flood Forecast and Alert System"
  Context: The Question (a) asserts AI has been applied incl. the "self-developed Hydrometric Information System"; the Reply itself does not describe the Hydrometric Information System as AI.

- **F02 | I2 / I5** — Hydrometric Information System (existing, self-developed): over 300 water level sensors and rain gauges, giving real-time monitoring of water levels in main rivers or drainage channels and rainfall across districts; data sent instantly to the system, viewable by DSD staff on mobile phones.
  Locator: S09 | PDF p.1 (printed p.328) | "Hydrometric Information System"
  Anchor: "comprises over 300 water level sensors"
  Context: Split between water-level sensors and rain gauges not given (combined ">300"). Monitoring network, not a hydraulic model. Not stated to be used as AI training data.

- **F03 | I2** — Since 2024, extended to road-flood monitoring via smart sensors called "Flood Monitoring Devices" along selected carriageways; more than 100 installed so far, measuring real-time water level on roads.
  Locator: S09 | PDF p.2 (printed p.329) | "Hydrometric Information System" (cont.)
  Anchor: "So far, more than 100 “Flood Monitoring Devices” have been installed"
  Context: Threshold-based alerting: when river/channel or road water level reaches warning level, devices immediately alert DSD and relevant departments; DSD deploys teams to inspect and clear blocked drains.

- **F04 | I1** — Cost of Hydrometric Information System + Flood Monitoring Devices: operational expenditure about $4 million in 2025-26; subsequent annual O&M estimated similar.
  Locator: S09 | PDF p.2 (printed p.329) | "Hydrometric Information System" (cont.)
  Anchor: "the operational expenditure of the Hydrometric Information System and the Flood Monitoring Devices was about $4 million"
  Context: Currency "$" only.

- **F05 | I3** — Mosaic Model Map (M³): developed 2024 by DSD in-house team as a "real-time territorial flood risk system" to overcome long computation times of traditional hydraulic models. Not explicitly labelled AI; no method, inputs or resolution given.
  Locator: S09 | PDF p.2 (printed p.329) | "Smart Flood Forecast and Alert System"
  Anchor: "developed the Mosaic Model Map (M³), a real-time territorial flood risk system, in 2024"
  Context: Identical wording to S08 F02.

- **F06 | I1 / I6** — M³ operational since 2025 for flood risk assessments, forecasting flood risks in coastal low-lying or windy residential areas ahead of Severe or Super Typhoons; developed/operated by staff, no additional costs.
  Locator: S09 | PDF p.2 (printed p.329) | "Smart Flood Forecast and Alert System"
  Anchor: "Since 2025, the system has been adopted for flood risk assessments"
  Context: Lead time qualitative only ("ahead of Severe or Super Typhoons").

- **F07 | I1 / I3** — Smart Flood Forecast and Alert System under development with South China University of Technology; integrates "big data and advanced AI technologies"; trials scheduled mid-2026 at 3 locations (Lam Tsuen River in Tai Po, Wan Chai, Tsui Ping River in Kwun Tong); development cost estimated around $1.3 million, covering operational expenditure at the 3 trial sites in 2026-27.
  Locator: S09 | PDF p.2 (printed p.329) | "Smart Flood Forecast and Alert System"
  Anchor: "The system is scheduled for trials in mid-2026 at 3 locations"
  Context: Pilot status. This is the only funding figure answering Question (b) — the Reply gives no separate "dedicated funding" amount and no expected reduction in response time. No AI architecture/inputs/lead time.

- **F08 | I1 / I3 / I5** — AI Flood Monitoring System: developed 2024 with HKUST using an AI large vision-language model; piloted at Chatham Road South (Tsim Sha Tsui), Tai Kiu Tsuen and Tung Tau Wai Village (Yuen Long); analyses road CCTV images to detect street flooding and instantly issues alerts for emergency-team deployment.
  Locator: S09 | PDF p.2 (printed p.329) | "AI Flood Monitoring System"
  Anchor: "by leveraging an AI large vision-language model"
  Context: Same as S08 F08–F10. Input = road CCTV imagery (operational); training data not described.

- **F09 | I1 / I6** — Expanded with HKPF: now connected to over 300 CCTV cameras under the HKPF "SmartView" initiative; development cost approx. $6 million, annual operational expenditure about $1.4 million.
  Locator: S09 | PDF p.2 (printed p.329) | "AI Flood Monitoring System"
  Anchor: "connected to over 300 CCTV cameras under the “SmartView” initiative of the HKPF"
  Context: Outcome qualitative ("enhancing the DSD's effectiveness in responding to extreme weather").

- **F10 | I6** — Combined operational outcome: DSD monitors river water levels, conducts flood forecasts and monitors carriageway flooding separately through the 3 systems; in 2025 most flooding cases could be resolved within 1 to 2 hours.
  Locator: S09 | PDF p.2 (printed p.329) | closing paragraph
  Anchor: "In 2025, most flooding cases could be resolved within 1 to 2 hours."
  Context: Not attributed to any single system, no baseline/before-after comparison, no count of cases; does not answer Question (a) on reduced flood losses. This is an operational response-time statistic, not a model accuracy metric.
